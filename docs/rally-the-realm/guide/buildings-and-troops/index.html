Buildings and Troops

The five buildings

Every building holds 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50 troops. The bigger it is, the bigger it looks.

  • House

    The everyday building. Grows foot soldiers. No special tricks, and plenty of them.

  • Castle

    Troops inside a castle are extra hard to beat. Attackers need about one and a half soldiers for every defender. The bonus stays in the castle: troops sent out of it are ordinary foot soldiers.

  • Forge

    Grows heavy soldiers. They hit one and a half times as hard, which is just enough to break through a castle’s defense one for one.

  • Rune tower

    Shoots any enemy soldier that walks through its circle, one at a time. Towers are magic: an empty tower still fires at full speed. Its troops only protect it from capture. Towers do not grow troops on their own, so fill them by sending reinforcements. Each upgrade makes the circle bigger and the shots faster. Gray towers shoot everyone.

  • Stable

    Grows riders, the fastest troops in the game. Speed means fewer of them get hit when they dash past a rune tower.

The three kinds of troops

  • Foot soldiers

    From houses and castles. The backbone of every army.

  • Heavy soldiers

    From forges. Hit harder, walk at the same speed as foot soldiers.

  • Riders

    From stables. Much faster on the road, but they hit like foot soldiers.

Troops change to match their new home

Troops always take on the type of the building they walk into. Send riders into a house, and later they come out as foot soldiers. Send foot soldiers into a forge, and they come out heavy.

Smart pairings

  • Forge vs castle: heavy soldiers are the best way to crack a castle.
  • Stable vs rune tower: riders race through a tower’s circle before it can do much damage.
  • Tower on a crossing: a rune tower beside a busy road wears down every attack that passes.
